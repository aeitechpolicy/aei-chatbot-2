	Comment to the Office of Science and Technology Policy: Regulatory Reform on Artificial Intelligence | American Enterprise Institute - AEI

Comments for FRN 2025-187373; Notice of Request for Information
Simply looking at the titles of former President Joe Biden’s and President Donald Trump’s AI policies reveal an overarching split in ideology. For Biden, the policy was meant to ensure “Safe, Secure, and Trustworthy Development and Use of Artificial Intelligence.” Trump’s goal with AI policy, in contrast, is about “Winning the Race.” Where the Biden administration talked about “responsible development” and “stakeholder engagement,” Trump’s new AI Action Plan cuts straight to the chase: America must achieve “dominance” in artificial intelligence.
This displays a shift toward competitive urgency, one that reflects a broader reckoning with how America approaches emerging technologies in an era of great power competition. That’s not to say that there is no mention of safety, security, and trustworthiness; it is just subsumed under the larger umbrella of innovation and competition.
The administration should be commended for the deregulatory actions it has taken since January 2025. Beginning with the rescission of Biden’s AI framework in Executive Order (EO) 14179 in late January and the Office of Management and Budget (OMB) Memos M-25-21 and M-25-22 in April, and then continuing in July with EO 14318 on data center permitting, the AI Action Plan, and EO 14319 on preventing woke AI in government, the administration has realigned AI to be focused on competition.
Ultimately, the success of these reforms will be judged by their impact, especially the extent to which they unleash American innovation. Disciplined implementation over the coming months will determine whether the actions taken so far will meet this test. The following four sections examine key areas where focused action can accelerate this competitive agenda.
