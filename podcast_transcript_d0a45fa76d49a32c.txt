As a result, in kind of more recent years, what we saw emerge — and this was a good 40-50 years ago, going back to the antitrust paradox — is this idea of the consumer welfare standard. We can create an objective measure to help us know in many of the cases where it's unclear whether or not competition laws are being violated that courts can use to examine whether or not consumers are being harmed by these anticompetitive acts. Because that's what's really at the heart of antitrust law. It's not designed to be about "is big bad?" It's designed to be about "are consumers being harmed and not receiving the full benefits of the market?"
